Made for big libraries

Your own videos, finally findable

No transcoding server, no photo-library tax, no “supported cloud only” — your files, presented properly no matter how deep the folders got.

Browse any SMB share

Works with Synology, QNAP, Windows, macOS, Linux — anything that speaks SMB. Find your share by name over Bonjour or type its address. The folders you sorted years ago, and the ones you never got round to, both open as they are.

Thumbnails that never slow down

Every thumbnail is generated once and never again — even at 5,000+ videos, browsing stays instant. Frames are kept on the TV and beside your files on the share, so a second device never rebuilds them.

Find it by what you remember

Tag a video from the TV or the Mac — no save step — and find it again by the tag, not the file name you forgot. Tags live in a small folder on your own share, so the Mac, the TV and every device pointed at it see the same ones.

Picks up where you stopped

Quit mid-video, come back days later — it offers to carry on from the exact spot. A 40-hour archive is watchable again when the TV remembers where you are in it.

On the Apple TV

No media server. Tag it once — it's already there.

The Apple TV app talks straight to the share you point it at — your files, over your own network, nothing in between. You need a share already: your NAS, or a folder your Mac shares. That's file sharing, not a media server.

No media server

Nothing to install on the NAS, no account anywhere, nothing exposed to the internet. The app speaks SMB directly — the same file sharing your Mac already does. Credentials stay in the tvOS Keychain, and the connection never leaves your local network.

Tag it once, on any screen

Tags and posters are written into a .FolderVideoPlayer folder on your own share. The TV reads every tag file it finds there and merges them — each person's tags in their own file, so writers never overwrite each other. What you labelled on the Mac is on the TV when you sit down.

Free · the management half

Where a big library gets managed

FolderVideoPlayer for macOS is free, and it reads the same shares your Apple TV does. Tag in bulk from a keyboard, clear out the duplicates eating your disk, and let it pre-build the thumbnails the TV shows — everything lands on the share, so both screens agree.

Plays a whole folder — QuickTime won't even play two

Open New… → pick a folder → it starts playing — every video in it, in order, subfolders included, looping until you stop it. Apple's built-in player can't even queue two files in a row; here the folder is the queue — no playlist to build, nothing to drag.

Tag from the Mac, see it on the TV

Type a tag and it is applied — no save step. Tags publish into the .FolderVideoPlayer folder on your share, so the Apple TV, a second Mac or an iPhone pointed at the same share sees them too.

Thumbnails made once, for every screen

The Mac writes the poster frames a library needs onto the share — and the Apple TV reads those same pictures instead of generating them over Wi-Fi. One library scanned on the Mac, instant browsing on the TV.

Tags itself: date, camera, place, quality

Point it at a folder and the tag suggestions come from what is already true — the folders you made, the date it was shot, which camera, 4K or 720p or Slow-mo, and the place for clips that carry GPS. It shows you what it would add before you apply it, and everything is computed on your Mac.

Finds duplicates in a huge library

Every video you play is fingerprinted as you watch — copies are then grouped with the keeper already chosen and the reason shown. Preview before you decide, tags from a discarded copy move to the one you keep, and nothing is ever deleted: duplicates go to the Trash, or to a folder you name on a share that has none.

Support

Questions, answered

What can FolderVideoPlayer play?
The video formats Apple TV supports natively — MP4, M4V and MOV — streamed straight from any SMB file share on your network. There is no transcoding and no bundled codecs: if tvOS itself can't decode the file, the app won't play it. Formats outside Apple's native support, such as MKV, WMV or DivX-encoded AVI, are not played.
Do I need an account, an internet connection or a NAS?
None of the three. There are no accounts and the app works fully offline on your local network. A NAS is the most common setup, but the share can live on a Mac, a Windows PC, a router with a USB drive — anything that serves SMB.
Will it slow down with a very large library?
No. Thumbnails are generated once and cached — on the TV and next to your files on the share — so browsing stays fast even in libraries of 5,000+ videos. A thumbnail that has been made is never made again.
Is this for my home videos and downloads — or for streaming?
Your own files: home movies, camera footage, family archives and the downloads you've kept — the personal library that outgrew folders. FolderVideoPlayer is not a streaming app and has nothing to do with any service; it only reads what you point it at on your own network, and nothing leaves home. If the library is big enough that finding, sorting and duplicate copies are the problem, that is exactly what the TV app and the free Mac companion are built to fix.
Can I use the same tags on my Mac and my Apple TV?
Yes. Tags and poster frames are stored in a .FolderVideoPlayer folder on your own share, so every device pointed at the same share sees the same tags — and the free Mac companion app can read and write them too.
Does my SMB account need read and write access?
Browsing and playback work with a read-only account. But to save tags and to share thumbnails between your devices, the app writes to a .FolderVideoPlayer folder on your share — so connect with an account that has read & write rights on the share.
